As you may remember from previous articles ([here](2026-06-29-ap-groups-part-1.md){ target=_blank }
and [here](2026-07-04-ap-groups-part-2.md){ target=_blank }), the way Raccoon displays group
accounts is a bit special.

## Forum Mode: simple by design

Instead of opening them in _Classic mode_ like regular accounts (with four sections: Posts, Posts &
Replies, Pinned, Media), groups are displayed in _Forum mode_ with a single section.

This layout[^1] has the purpose of giving prominence to the "aggregator-like" feature of Friendica,
and the screen structure is intentionally simple.

The screen title displays the group name, while the main content is a refreshable list of top-level
posts, each of which is the root topic of a discussion thread. Full stop.

## Spotting the missing bit

In my main Friendica instance, I have a group account which I use to communicate news and receive
feedback. I check it out from time to time to see whether there are bug reports or feature requests.

I don't know how it could have happened… probably because I've always had more important aspects to
focus on, but it was not until very recently that I realized _Forum mode_ was missing a crucial bit.

There are basically two ways a user can interact with a group:

1. **Participating in the discussion** by either:
    - creating a new thread, which translates to creating a new top-level post mentioning the
      group;[^2]
    - adding a reply to an existing thread;
2. **Joining the group**, which translates to following the account.

In Raccoon, the first kind of interaction had been implemented, documented and optimized. But what
about the second one?

No button, no dedicated item in the top app bar actions menu, nothing. Absolutely nothing. 

The only way to actually join the group was (ironically) to switch back to _Classic mode_ and use
the relationship button which is shown for regular accounts.

## Small details, big impact

In order to solve this quite important usability flaw, I chose to implement a header card, very
similar to the one used for regular users, except that it is vertically more compact[^3] to keep the
focus on the discussion.

It is weird that I am only realizing this after almost two years, of project development.

However, since groups are a minority of accounts in the current Fediverse, this oversight could go
unnoticed.

The telltale sign was a user comment asking to make _Classic mode_ the default, which got me
wondering why: what could make a user miss the classic view?

This is part of a continuous effort to make the UI more functional and appealing. I've also recently
restyled the app info dialog to have a "fresher" layout (more concise texts, custom logos, etc.).

I am also trying to make interactions with groups easier, literally "a touch of a finger away" for
everyone, because it is one of the most powerful and interesting features of the Fediverse.

[^1]: Which can be disabled in Settings by turning off "Open groups in forum mode by default".
[^2]: With either an @-style public mention (Mastodon & Friendica) or a !-style private mention
(Friendica-only).
[^3]: The possibility to enable/disable notifications was placed in the top app bar action menu.
